Privacy · Where your data lives

Where Murmur’s data goes.

Recordings, history, your dictionary and the words you type stay on your Mac. With cloud recognition, the audio goes straight to the recognition provider for the tier you picked, not through our servers. When you are signed in and have not entered your own key, the text for cleanup, translation, Ask and voice editing goes through our servers first and then to the model vendor; our servers do not store it. Below, for each kind of data: where it goes, where it is stored, and how to delete it, each with a way to check it yourself.

Journey · Where each kind of data goes

Six kinds of data. Six journeys.

What it isWhere it goes · where it sitsHow to delete it · how to switch it off
Recordingsyour voice

During recognition the audio goes straight to the one provider behind the tier you picked: on free-allowance cloud recognition that is Soniox (United States); with your own key, it is the provider whose key you entered. Pick on-device recognition and the audio stays on this computer; for a few languages where macOS has no offline recognition, Apple may process it. Recording files sit on your Mac, kept 30 days by default so you can re-run them, then deleted automatically.

Settings can cut that to 7 days, or to “don’t keep them at all”. Delete the local folder and they are gone for real. With on-device recognition the audio stays on your Mac; with cleanup on, the text still goes to a language model. To keep what you say entirely on your Mac: pick on-device recognition, set cleanup to “Off (raw transcript)”, and don’t use translation, Ask or voice editing.

Transcript and cleaned-up textwhat you actually said

With AI cleanup on, or when you use translation, Ask or voice editing, the transcript is handed to a language model once (voice editing also sends the passage you selected). With your own key it goes straight to that vendor; signed in without a key, on the free allowance, it passes through our servers and is processed by DeepSeek (China); our servers do not store it. “Where did this go?” on the app’s History page shows which path your current settings use. With prompt skills on, a long enough sentence you say with the dictation key in an AI tool is sent one extra time over the same channel to the same vendor, to decide what kind of request it is; no audio is sent, and which window you are in is not sent. The finished text and your history live in a database on your Mac and are not synced to any cloud as a whole. “Personal style” is off by default. Once it is on, each cleanup picks up to 3 of your earlier “what you said → cleaned-up text” pairs from recent dictation history and sends them with the cleanup request as samples.

Switch cleanup off (the “raw transcript, untouched” setting) and the text reaches no language model. History can be deleted one entry at a time. To clear it all at once, go to Settings → System, set “Keep text” to “Don’t keep”, then click “Apply the retention rules now”. You set how long it is kept.

Dictionaryyour proper nouns

So it hears the names and terms you say often, the words in your dictionary are sent along with every recognition request; with cleanup on, they also go with the cleanup request to the language model. That is written in plain sight in Settings, not buried in terms.

Delete an entry whenever you like; once deleted it stops being sent.

Typed wordswords the pinyin keyboard has learned

They are stored on this Mac at ~/Library/Application Support/Murmur/lexicon.sqlite. Learning is on by default. Password fields and apps on the deny list are never recorded. When you dictate, these words go to the recognition provider as hints, sent with each recognition request. This is the same path as the Dictionary row above. Pick on-device recognition and they do not leave the Mac. The first version does not sync across devices. When you press ⌥⇧Space for the AI cleanup card, the words the sentence touches from your dictionary and from what you typed go with that request (at most 500). This happens with on-device recognition too.

Settings → Input Method → the "Words I've typed (learned by the input method)" card: turn off "Learn the words I type" and no new words are learned after that; turn off "Let AI use my own words" and the AI cleanup card sends none of your words. Settings → System → "Clear data…" wipes the learned words in one go. The "Suggest the next word (from what you've typed)" switch sits on the same card.

Screen contexttext in the frontmost window at the moment you record

At the moment you start speaking it reads the visible text of the frontmost window once (through Accessibility, no screenshot), to get proper nouns right. With cloud recognition, the proper nouns taken from it go to the recognition provider with the audio. With cleanup on, or in translate and Ask, an excerpt of the screen text (1500 characters at most) goes to the model vendor with the transcript. On the free allowance it passes through our server first; with your own key it goes straight to that vendor. On your Mac it is never written to the database, to disk or to a log, and words from your screen never enter your dictionary.

On by default. Turn off “Use the text on screen to get proper nouns right” in Settings → Recognition. With on-device recognition and cleanup off it is never read in the first place.

Sign-in, API keys and settingsyour credentials

Your sign-in credentials are kept in the macOS Keychain. If you entered your own API key, it is in the Keychain too, on this Mac only, and we never receive it. Settings are kept in system preferences.

Deleting the app cleans them up the way macOS normally does; keys can be cleared in Settings at any time.

Never · What we don’t do

In writing, so you can hold us to it.

✕

We never handle your audio. With cloud recognition the audio goes from your Mac straight to the recognition provider, through none of our servers. Text for cleanup is an exception; see “Once you sign in” below.

✕

No behavioural tracking. There is no analytics SDK in the app. Which app you were speaking into is never reported back to us. Once you sign in, each sentence (on-device ones included) reports its character count, how long you spoke and which path it took, to count your allowance, with no text attached; see “Once you sign in” below.

✕

We don’t sell your data. It goes only to the processors needed to run the service (listed under “Once you sign in” below). There are no advertisers. Your data is not the product.

✕

No analytics on this website. No tracking, no ads. The sign-in, try-it and skills pages serve their scripts from this site; only when abuse protection is on do they load Cloudflare’s check, listed under “Website” below; open your browser’s network panel and check.

Verify · Don’t take our word for it. Look.

Three things you can check yourself, right now.

① Your data is sitting in this folder

~/Library/Application Support/Murmur/ — the recordings and every word you have ever said to it are in there. Open it whenever you want. Delete it and it is really deleted.

② Where the app connects

Point a free firewall like LuLu at Murmur and watch its outbound connections. You will see these: the recognition and language-model providers you picked (plus the web-search provider, if you entered your own key for it); our server (sign-in, allowance, public config, one-time passes for free-allowance recognition, and forwarding cleanup text); this website (a version file, once a day); GitHub when you download the Rime dictionary or a sentence model; and when you open the “Cleanup” page in Settings, one read of OpenRouter’s public model list, with none of your content.

③ Every single entry says where it went

“Where did this go?” on the app’s History page reports it honestly, against your current settings: which recognition tier handled this entry, whether the text passed through a language model, whether screen context was on.

Account · Once you sign in

Even when you are not signed in, the app connects to our server to read a public config: only while the app is in front or within 5 minutes after you dictate, at an interval the server sets between 60 and 3600 seconds (5 minutes by default). That request carries neither who you are nor anything you said. It also reads a version file on this website once a day.

When you are signed in, our servers also do these things:

① Sign-in, allowance and the public config (signed in, it comes with your account). ② After each sentence, one report: character count, how long you spoke, dictation or Ask, which path it took (on-device / your own key / free allowance) and when it happened. On-device sentences are reported too and use no allowance. No text attached. ③ On the free allowance, each recording fetches a one-time pass, so the server knows you are recording at that moment; the audio still goes straight to the recognition provider. ④ On the free allowance (no key of your own), the text for cleanup, translation, Ask and voice editing goes through the server and then to the model vendor. The same request carries your dictionary and the screen excerpt (1500 characters at most); with personal style on, up to 3 sample sentences from your history; with prompt skills on, the sentence said in an AI tool is sent one extra time. Our servers only forward it and count the cost; they do not store the text, and no text goes into the logs. ⑤ With cloud candidates on in the input method (off by default), Pinyin you have not committed goes through our servers to DeepSeek (China); only the Pinyin is sent.

Recordings are not uploaded. History is not uploaded as a whole; with personal style on, a cleanup request carries up to 3 entries. Your dictionary goes out whole with recognition and cleanup requests, as in the “Dictionary” row above. The words you type are not uploaded as a whole; only the ones a sentence touches go with a ⌥⇧Space AI cleanup card request, as described in the “Typed words” row above. Every change to this page leaves its date at the bottom.

Who processes it, and where. On the free allowance, the text for cleanup, translation, Ask and voice editing is processed by DeepSeek (China); Pinyin sent for cloud candidates in the input method is processed by DeepSeek (China); cloud recognition audio goes to Soniox (United States); your account and usage are stored with Supabase (Singapore); email is sent through Resend. With your own key, the text or audio goes straight to the provider whose key you entered.

What our servers keep, and for how long. Our servers keep: your email address; the allowance ledger (time, character count, speaking time, type and path of each entry); your subscription status; and the request-a-seat form (email, message, page language, a salted hash of your network address and a short browser type). We keep these until 30 days after you delete your account, then delete them. To delete your account or get a copy of your data, write to contact@maobenbeginner.com.

If a data breach affects you, we will tell you promptly.
