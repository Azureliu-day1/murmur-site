Privacy · Where your data lives

A privacy page you can check yourself,
not terms you have to sign.

Your recordings, history and dictionary stay on your Mac. For cloud recognition, the audio goes straight to the recognition provider behind the tier you picked, not through our servers. When you are signed in and have not entered your own key, the text for cleanup, translation, Ask and voice editing passes through our servers on its way to the language model; our servers do not store it. Below, for every kind of data: where it goes, where it sits, how to delete it — and for each one, how to verify it with your own hands.

Journey · Where each kind of data goes

Five kinds of data. Five journeys.

What it isWhere it goes · where it sitsHow to delete it · how to switch it off
Recordingsyour voice

During recognition the audio goes straight to the one provider behind the tier you picked — which provider sits behind which tier is stated live in the app’s own “Privacy” panel (it is updated as the tiers change, so it is newer than this page). Pick on-device recognition and not one byte of audio leaves this computer. Recording files sit on your Mac, kept 30 days by default so you can re-run them, then deleted automatically.

Settings can cut that to 7 days, or to “don’t keep them at all”. Delete the local folder and they are gone for real. With on-device recognition the audio stays on your Mac; with cleanup on, the text still goes to a language model. To keep what you say entirely on your Mac: pick on-device recognition, set cleanup to “Off (raw transcript)”, and don’t use translation, Ask or voice editing.

Transcript and cleaned-up textwhat you actually said

With AI cleanup on, or when you use translation, Ask or voice editing, the transcript is handed to a language model once (voice editing also sends the passage you selected). With your own key it goes straight to that provider; signed in without a key, on the free allowance, it passes through our servers first, which do not store it. The app’s own “Privacy” panel shows which path your current settings use. The finished text and your history live in a database on your Mac and are not synced to any cloud as a whole. “Personal style” is off by default. Turn it on and each cleanup picks up to 3 “what you said → cleaned-up text” pairs from your recent dictation as examples and sends them with the cleanup request.

Switch cleanup off (the “raw transcript, untouched” setting) and the text reaches no language model. History can be deleted one entry at a time or wiped entirely, and you set how long it is kept.

Dictionaryyour proper nouns

So it hears the names and terms you say often, the words in your dictionary are sent along with every recognition request; with cleanup on, they also go with the cleanup request to the language model. That is written in plain sight in Settings, not buried in terms.

Delete an entry whenever you like; once deleted it stops being sent.

Screen contexttext in the frontmost window at the moment you record

At the moment you start speaking it reads the visible text of the frontmost window once (through Accessibility, no screenshot), to get proper nouns right. With a cloud recognition tier, the proper nouns it pulls out go with the audio to the recognition provider. With cleanup on, or in translation and Ask, an excerpt of the screen text (1500 characters at most) goes with the transcript to the language model: through our servers on the free allowance, straight to the provider with your own key. On your Mac it is never written to the database, to disk or to a log, and words from your screen never enter your dictionary.

On by default. Turn off “Use the text on screen to get proper nouns right” in Settings → Recognition. With on-device recognition and cleanup off it is never read in the first place.

API keys and settingsyour credentials

API keys sit in the macOS Keychain (system-level encryption), settings in system preferences — both on this machine only. We cannot see them and never receive them.

Deleting the app cleans them up the way macOS normally does; keys can be cleared in Settings at any time.

Never · What we don’t do

In writing, so you can hold us to it.

✕

We never handle your audio. Cloud recognition audio goes from your Mac straight to the recognition provider, through none of our servers. Text for cleanup is the exception; see “Once you sign in” below.

✕

No behavioural tracking. There is no analytics SDK in the app, and which app you were speaking into is never reported back to us. Once you sign in, each sentence that used a cloud service reports its character count (long recordings report their length) to count your allowance, with no text attached; see “Once you sign in” below.

✕

No selling your data, no sharing it. There are no advertisers and no “partners”. Your data is not the product.

✕

No analytics on this website. No tracking, no ads. The sign-in and try-it pages load third-party scripts for sign-in and abuse protection, listed under “Website” below; open your browser’s network panel and check.

Verify · Don’t take our word for it. Look.

Three things you can check yourself, right now.

① Your data is sitting in this folder

~/Library/Application Support/Murmur/ — the recordings and every word you have ever said to it are in there. Open it whenever you want. Delete it and it is really deleted.

② Where the app connects

Point a free firewall like LuLu at Murmur and watch its outbound connections. You will see these: the recognition and language-model providers you picked (plus the web-search provider, if you entered your own key for it); once you sign in, our servers (sign-in, allowance, one-time passes for free-allowance recognition, and forwarding cleanup text); and when you open the “Cleanup” page in Settings, one read of OpenRouter’s public model list, with none of your content.

③ Every single entry says where it went

“Where did this go?” on the app’s History page reports it honestly, against your current settings: which recognition tier handled this entry, whether the text passed through a language model, whether screen context was on.

Account · Once you sign in

If you are not signed in, the app does not connect to our servers.

Once you sign in, our servers do these things:

① Sign-in, allowance and subscription status. ② After each sentence that used a cloud service, one report of its character count and type (dictation or Ask; long recordings report their length), with no text attached. ③ On the free allowance, each recording starts by fetching a one-time pass for recognition, so our servers know you are recording at that moment; the audio still goes straight to the recognition provider. ④ On the free allowance (no key of your own), the text for cleanup, translation, Ask and voice editing passes through our servers on its way to the language model. The same request carries your dictionary and the screen excerpt, and with personal style on, up to 3 past example sentences. Our servers only forward it and count the cost; they do not store the text, and no text goes into the logs.

Recordings are never uploaded. History is never uploaded as a whole; with personal style on, a cleanup request carries up to 3 entries. Every change to this page leaves its date at the bottom.

Website

This website has no analytics scripts and no ads. These are the places where something goes to our servers or a third party:

① The request-a-seat form stores your email, the line you wrote and the page language, plus a salted hash of your network address used only to limit how often it can be submitted. ② Trying cleanup on the web sends the text you paste to our servers, which pass it to a language model and do not store it. ③ The sign-in and try-it pages load the sign-in script from cdn.jsdelivr.net; when abuse protection is switched on, those two pages also load Cloudflare Turnstile.
