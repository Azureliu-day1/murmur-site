Privacy Policy

Covers Murmur 0.53. Updated 13 October 2026.

In short

  • Your recordings, history and dictionary are kept on your own device, for as long as you choose in Settings.
  • When you’re signed out, your speech is turned into text by the recognition built into your device’s system, not sent to us.
  • When you’re signed in, turning your speech into text, tidying it up and translating means that sentence goes to the speech recognition and text processing services we use. Those services are in the United States and China; our account database is in Singapore. Section 5 lists what each kind of service handles.
  • We don’t store what you said. When you’re signed in, we record your email, sign-in details and usage (word count, duration and time), never the content.
  • With screen context on, the app reads the text in the front window once when you start speaking, to get names and terms right, including other people’s messages in that window. It is off on new installs, and an upgrade keeps your earlier choice; switch it in Settings › Recognition.
  • We don’t sell your data, and there are no ads or third-party analytics in the app.
  • To see, export, correct or delete your data, or to ask anything, write to support@day1aifitness.com.

This policy applies to the Murmur apps on every platform and the Murmur website, provided by Others Studio (Victoria, Australia). Others Studio is responsible for the personal information described here.

Where your data goes depends on the feature, so each one is covered separately below. Some of it depends on your settings; section 11 explains how to check this in the app.

3What each feature sends

3.1On-device recognition

With on-device recognition, transcription happens on your Mac and the audio is not sent to us or to any recognition provider. There is one exception: if your macOS version can’t recognise the current language offline, the system may send the audio to the operating system provider, under its privacy policy.

3.2Cloud recognition

  • On the free allowance, audio goes from your Mac to the speech recognition provider (United States). Each time you start recording, our server issues a single-use temporary credential, so we know when you start recording; for dictation and live captions on the Mac, the audio does not pass through our servers.
  • With your own service key, audio goes directly to that provider.
  • Sent along with each recognition request: words from your dictionary, words learned by the input method, and, if screen context is on, up to 40 proper nouns taken from the text on screen. These help the recogniser get names and terms right.
  • Long recordings are transcribed with speakers identified. On the free allowance, long recordings on the Mac stream to the speech recognition provider in real time, like dictation; for whole-file transcription of long recordings from your phone or Watch, the recording is held briefly in our cloud storage (Singapore), fetched by the speech recognition provider, and deleted as soon as transcription finishes, and no later than 3 hours after transcription starts. With your own key, the whole recording is uploaded to that provider as a file, and the app asks the provider to delete it once transcription is done. If the app quits during transcription, the file may be kept for the provider’s own retention period.

3.3Cleanup, translation, and voice commands

These features send the transcript to a language model once:

  • Cleanup, which turns spoken text into written text;
  • Translation;
  • Ask, which treats what you say as an instruction and inserts the AI’s answer. Any text you have selected is sent too;
  • Voice editing, which changes the text you have selected according to what you say, and sends that selection.

Where the text goes:

  • On the free allowance, the text goes to our server and then to the text processing provider (China). Our server only forwards and meters it. It does not keep the text, and the text does not appear in our logs.
  • With your own service key, the text goes to the provider you added the key for; some pass it on to the model provider you chose.
  • If web search is on for Ask and you have added your own search service key, your search queries go to that service.

The same request may also include:

  • your dictionary;
  • if screen context is on, up to 1,500 characters of on-screen text;
  • if Personal style is on (it is off by default), up to 3 examples of what you said and how it was cleaned up, taken from your recent history.

Prompt skills tidies up prompts you dictate into AI tools. When it is on, a longer sentence is sent once more to the same provider to work out what kind of request it is. Only the text is sent, with no audio and no window details.

Even with on-device recognition, cleanup still sends text to a language model. To keep everything you say on your Mac, choose on-device recognition, set cleanup to Off, and don’t use translation, Ask, or voice editing.

3.4Input method

  • Words the input method learns are stored on this Mac only.
  • When you press the shortcut to have AI clean up the current sentence, the related words from your dictionary and learned words (up to 500) go with the request, including when you use on-device recognition. Turning off “Let AI use my words” in Settings › Input Method stops this entirely.
  • Cloud candidates are off by default. When on, pinyin you have not yet confirmed goes through our server to the text processing provider (China). Only the pinyin is sent.

3.5Screen context

  • Off by default on new installs. If you upgraded from an earlier version, your previous choice is kept. Turn it on or off in Settings › Recognition.
  • When on, Murmur reads the visible text of the front window once, at the moment you start recording. It uses macOS accessibility features and takes no screenshot. If the front app is on your exclusion list, macOS secure input is active, or the cursor is in a password field, nothing in the window is read.
  • With cloud recognition, proper nouns taken from that text go to the recognition provider with the audio.
  • With cleanup on, or when you use translation or Ask, up to 1,500 characters of it go to the language model provider with the transcript. On the free allowance it goes through our server; with your own key it goes to the provider for that key.
  • The text is not stored or logged on your Mac, and words from the screen are never added to your dictionary. With on-device recognition and cleanup off, nothing is read.

4Records on our servers

Our servers keep:

  • Account. Your email address, account status, and allowance.
  • Sign-in sessions. Network address and device or browser information, used to keep your account secure.
  • Usage records. For each sentence you dictate (including on-device ones, which don’t use allowance), the character count, how long you spoke, the feature, the path it took (on-device, your own key, or the free allowance), and the time. We use these to calculate allowance and prevent abuse. They don’t include anything you said.
  • Language model forwarding records. For each request we forward, the tier, model, provider, estimated usage, and cost. No text.
  • Beta place applications. See section 6.
  • Invite code attempts. Network address, email address, the first characters of the code, and the result, used to prevent invite code abuse. Kept for 30 days.
  • Email delivery records. The delivery status of the email that tells you your beta place is ready.
  • Request logs. Time, route, status, duration, and account ID. No email address, content, or keys.

Even when you are not signed in, the app reads public configuration from time to time and checks for a new version once a day. These requests carry no identity and none of your content.

How long we keep it. When you delete your account, we delete your account, sign-in sessions, usage records, email delivery records, application, and invite code attempts from our live systems within 30 days. Request logs are deleted automatically under our hosting provider’s retention period. Copies in backups are overwritten when the backup cycle ends.

  • Where requests are handled. The database and the temporary recording files for whole-file transcription are in Singapore. Requests from the app and the website may also pass through the country of the server nearest you.

6Information the website collects

  • Beta place applications. Your email address, the message you write, the interface language, and your browser type, plus a one-way hash of your network address used only to limit repeat submissions. Submitting sets up an account for that email in advance, and the form is forwarded to our inbox through our email provider.
  • Trying cleanup on the website. The text you paste goes through our server to the text processing provider (China). Our server does not keep it. We also keep a daily one-way hash of your network address to limit the number of tries.
  • Scripts. Scripts on the sign-in, try, and skills pages are hosted on this site. When bot protection is on, those three pages also load a script from our bot protection provider.
  • Cookies and local storage. The website uses no advertising or analytics cookies. Once you sign in, your sign-in state is stored in your browser. The bot protection provider may set the cookies it needs to work.
  • Do Not Track. We don’t track you across websites, so we don’t respond differently to a browser’s Do Not Track signal.
  • Hosting. The website is hosted by our website hosting provider, which receives standard access information such as your network address when you visit.

7Legal bases

If you are in the European Economic Area or the UK, we rely on these legal bases:

  • Providing the features you use (recognition, cleanup, your account, allowance): performance of our contract with you.
  • Usage metering, abuse prevention, sign-in sessions, and request logs: our legitimate interest in keeping the service secure and fair.
  • Beta place applications: steps taken at your request, when you apply, before entering into a contract.
  • Responding to legal requirements: compliance with a legal obligation.

10Data breaches and changes to this policy

  • If a data breach is likely to affect you, we will notify you promptly and report it to regulators as applicable law requires.
  • Each update to this policy is dated at the top of the page. For significant changes to how existing features handle your data that are to your disadvantage, we will email registered users at least 30 days before they take effect and show a notice in the app.
  • Information about how a new feature handles data takes effect when that feature launches and applies to people who start using it.

11Checking these claims yourself

  • Files on your Mac. Everything stored locally is in ~/Library/Application Support/Murmur/, and you can open it at any time. Deleting that folder deletes the copy on your Mac; records on our servers and with providers are handled as described in sections 4 and 5.
  • Network connections. A network monitor such as LuLu shows the addresses Murmur connects to. To check which kind of provider each address belongs to, email us.
  • Where your data went. In the app’s History, “Where did my data go” uses your current settings to show what a dictation sends and where.
