Your recordings and history
are kept on your own device.

By default, recordings are kept for 30 days and history until you clear it. You can change both in Settings or delete them at any time.

Once you sign in, each sentence you say is sent to the service providers that handle speech recognition and text processing for us, and the result comes back to you. We don't keep a copy of what you said, and we don't sell your data.

When “Use the text on screen to get proper nouns right” is on, Murmur reads the text in the front window once as you start speaking, so it can spell names and terms correctly. It doesn't take screenshots, and what it reads isn't stored on your device. When you're signed in, that text is sent to the same providers along with your sentence. The setting is off by default; you can turn it on in Settings.

The privacy page lists the kinds of providers involved, the countries they are in, and how long data is kept →

If it goes wrong, the recording is still there.

Offline, or out of allowance

The signal drops on the train. Murmur switches to a fallback way of recognising speech and keeps the recording. It is slower then, and less accurate when you mix languages. Once you are back online, you can run it again from your history.

It misheard you

Every sentence keeps its recording. Run it again from your history, or correct the text yourself. Murmur takes your corrections into account next time.

No sound

If the microphone picks up nothing or a translation fails, you are told on screen right away, and nothing is typed into your window.

Lectures, meetings, calls in another language.
Text appears as you record.

Start a long recording and the text appears on the recording page as people speak, whether it is an online lecture, a video meeting or a meeting in another language. When you stop, the full transcript goes into your history, split by speaker so you can tell who said what. If you use your own recognition service key, the captions can also float above all other windows, even over a full-screen lecture, and for a meeting in another language you can open a second column with the translation. The captions are only a preview; the recording keeps running underneath.

When you can't speak out loud.

In a meeting room or a library, you still need to type. Murmur includes a Pinyin input method that shares one dictionary with dictation: names you have taught it to hear show up as candidates when you type, and words you type help it recognise you when you speak.

Frequently asked questions

How is it different from built-in dictation?

Built-in dictation writes down what you said, word for word. Murmur turns it into clean written text: filler words removed, word order straightened out, punctuation added, and sentences that mix Chinese and English written correctly. Tidying up requires you to sign in.

How big is the free allowance?

Once you sign in, 2,000 words a week (each Chinese character counts as one), shared between dictation and tidying up. When this week’s allowance is used up, you can keep dictating, and Murmur writes down exactly what you said without tidying it. Accounts are by request for now.

What does signing in add?

Tidying up sentences, removing filler words, mixing Chinese and English in one sentence, translating, asking questions, and rewriting selected text by voice. These draw on the free allowance. Signed out, Murmur recognises one language at a time and writes down exactly what you said.

Which apps does it work in?

Text goes wherever your cursor is, with no plugin to install for each app. It works in mail, chat apps, the terminal and code editors. It types at the cursor using the macOS Accessibility permission, which it asks for the first time you open it.

Which languages does it support?

English and Chinese. Once you sign in, you can switch between them mid-sentence without changing any setting, and English words inside Chinese stay in English. Signed out, it recognises one language at a time.

Where are my recordings and text stored?

Recordings and history stay on your own device. By default, recordings are kept for 30 days and history until you clear it; you can change both in Settings or delete them at any time. Once you sign in, each sentence is sent to the providers that turn speech into text and tidy it up. We don’t keep what you said, and we don’t sell your data. The privacy page explains how Murmur reads the front window, where those providers are based and how long data is kept.

What do I need to run it?

A Mac with Apple silicon running macOS 26 or later. The download is about 45 MB. Intel Macs and earlier versions of macOS aren’t supported yet. A Windows version is in testing and expected within two months.
