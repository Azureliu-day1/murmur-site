Pinyin · Chinese input method

A Pinyin keyboard
comes with Murmur.

Murmur ships a Pinyin input method for the Mac. Requires macOS 26 or later.

01
One vocabulary for speaking and typing.Names in your dictation dictionary show up as candidates. Words you type are recognised when you speak.
02
Wrong word? Press Delete.Within 1.5 seconds of a commit, one Delete turns the whole word back into Pinyin.
03
⌥⇧Space opens the AI cleanup card.Press it before the Pinyin commits. AI fixes punctuation and word order and shows the result on a card first.
04
Cloud candidates, off by default.Press ⌥↩ and the cloud suggests a whole sentence. Sign-in required. The text next to the switch says what is sent.

First-choice accuracy on our own set of 125 spoken phrases not in the dictionary: 80.0%, and 89.6% with the language model installed.

Install: Settings → Input Method → Install. How to switch to it and how each feature works are in the guide.

Local · Where your data is

Recording files stay
on your Mac.

Your recordings, transcripts, history, dictionary and typed words are stored on your Mac. For cloud recognition the audio goes straight from your Mac to the provider you picked, not through us. With on-device recognition the audio stays on this computer.

Signed in on the free allowance, the text for cleanup, translation, Ask and voice editing passes through our server on its way to the language model. Our server does not store it. With cleanup on, the text still goes to a language model even with on-device recognition. Proper nouns from your dictionary go with the request so they are heard correctly. With cloud candidates on in the input method, Pinyin you have not committed also goes through our server. The AI cleanup card (⌥⇧Space) sends the words from your vocabulary that the sentence touches. All of this is listed in Settings.

You speak, and the audio leaves from your Mac

~/Library/Application Support/Murmur/

Promises · In writing

Four promises.

What you said is kept

Every dictation saves the audio and a history entry. If the cloud fails or the text is wrong, run it again on another engine from the history.

Errors are reported

If a translation fails, you see a message. If the microphone heard nothing, you see that too. It does not paste garbage into your window.

You can see where audio goes

With on-device recognition, audio stays on this computer. With cloud recognition, Settings shows which provider got it and what was sent.

The numbers are measured

If a tier mishears you, switch to another one on the “Models” page of the main window.

Lecture · Long recordings

Record a lecture.

Start a long recording and a caption window floats over the full-screen lecture as it plays. Turn on the second column to see a translation beside the captions. The captions are only a preview, and the recording continues if they drop. When you stop, the full transcript goes into your history with the speakers told apart.

Details · Small features

Smaller features.

The dictionary adds words. Proper nouns you have corrected go into the dictionary and are heard right next time. Words it is unsure about are left out.
Quick phrases. Say a trigger word and a whole address or a stock reply lands. This step does not go through a large model.
Tone per app. Text that lands in an email is more formal than text in a chat app. In a code editor your identifiers are left alone.
Fix mistakes in the history. Every dictation keeps its original text and its audio. Words you correct are used as a reference from then on.
Whisper mode. Whisper in a library and it amplifies the sound before recognising it.
“Remind me in half an hour.” Say it in Ask (fn) and the reminder is set and pops up on time. It runs on-device, uses no allowance and works offline. Ask is off by default.
Names on your screen help it hear right. When you start recording, it reads the visible text of the front window once to find proper nouns. It takes no screenshot, and those words never enter your dictionary. Off by default; turn it on in Settings → Recognition. Details are in “Where your data lives”.
Questions · FAQ
Where does my audio get sent?

Your recordings, transcripts, history, dictionary and typed words are stored on your Mac, and for cloud recognition the audio goes straight to the recognition service, not through our server. What text requests, cloud candidates and the AI cleanup card send is listed under “Where your data is” above and on the privacy page.

What happens when I am offline?

When you are offline or out of allowance, it switches to on-device recognition by itself. On-device recognition is slower and less accurate with mixed languages, and the recording is still saved. Once you are back online, you can run any history entry again on a cloud tier.

Which languages does it handle?

English, Chinese, and the two mixed inside one sentence. Chinese with a Cantonese, Sichuanese or Wu accent has its own tier, currently in closed beta.

Do I have to bring my own API key?

No. Cloud allowance is invite-only for now. Once you have a seat and sign in, the free allowance is there, and we pay its cloud bill. When it runs out, the app switches to on-device recognition and does not ask you to pay.
